## Example 1: The School Climate Code

**Subjects:** geography, physics, politics, mathematics  
**Grade:** 8-10  
**Genre:** documentary / investigation  
**Idea:** A class investigates where energy is lost in everyday school life and
turns measurements, interviews, and observations into a concrete climate action
plan.

### Story

Class 8b starts with a simple question: Why does the school use so much energy
even when nobody wants to waste it? Measurement logs, facility-management
interviews, cafeteria observations, and a climate council become a fictional
streaming documentary. Each episode shows a different step: measure, interpret,
evaluate, decide.

### Episodes

1. **The Electricity Detective** - standby consumption, lighting, device use.
2. **Heat on the Run** - temperature maps, windows, radiators, routines.
3. **The Cafeteria Balance** - food waste, transport routes, regional options.
4. **The Data Proof** - charts, costs, interviews, arguments.
5. **The Climate Council** - comfort, cost, fairness, feasibility.
6. **A Plan for Monday** - immediate action and follow-up questions.

**Episode 1:** Students measuring classroom devices with a plug-in power meter, realistic documentary photo, no text, 16:9.

**Episode 2:** Thermal-camera-inspired view of classroom windows and radiators, students comparing notes, no text, 16:9.

**Episode 3:** School cafeteria with students weighing food waste and checking regional ingredients, no text, 16:9.

**Episode 4:** Students analyzing charts and spreadsheets on a classroom screen, no text, 16:9.

**Episode 6:** Students arranging action cards on a classroom board, practical planning mood, no text, 16:9.

## Example 2: The Weimar File

**Subjects:** history, language arts, politics  
**Grade:** 10-12  
**Genre:** historical report / docudrama  
**Idea:** A student editorial team reconstructs the Weimar Republic as an
investigative series and asks how democracy remains stable.

### Story

A history class treats the Weimar Republic not as a timeline, but as a file.
Each episode investigates one crisis or turning point: revolution, constitution,
inflation, culture, radicalization, and the collapse of democratic institutions.
The final question connects the past with the present: What does democracy need
to remain resilient?

### Episodes

1. **A Republic Begins** - revolution, constitution, new order.
2. **Money Made of Paper** - inflation and everyday experience.
3. **Golden Years?** - culture, science, contradictions.
4. **Streets Full of Slogans** - political radicalization.
5. **The Last Edition** - collapse, responsibility, present-day relevance.

### Image prompts

**Cover:** Cinematic historical classroom investigation board about the Weimar Republic, old newspapers, timeline strings, student researchers in a modern school setting, sepia and neutral colors, no readable text, no logos, 16:9.

**Episode 1:** Students examining historical photos and a map of Germany in a classroom archive corner, realistic documentary style, no readable text, 16:9.

**Episode 2:** Close-up of students comparing old banknotes, price tags, and handwritten notes on inflation, historical learning project, realistic, no readable text, 16:9.

**Episode 3:** School presentation scene with Bauhaus-inspired shapes, theater masks, science posters, students discussing culture of the 1920s, no text, 16:9.

**Episode 4:** Dramatic classroom debate scene with students sorting political posters into categories, tense but educational, realistic, no readable text, 16:9.

**Episode 5:** Students recording a final podcast-style reflection at a classroom table with archive material and microphones, serious democratic education mood, no text, 16:9.
